# Ashfall: 24 Hours Survival Under a Volcano (Part 1)
*Short version: about 3 minutes, about 500 words. Narrator: calm, serious, talks to "you".*

---

## COLD OPEN

If you ever hear a roar coming down a dry riverbed, don't look back. Run uphill.

**[DAY 60]**

The ground under your boots starts to hum.
Your dog won't move. His ears are straight up.
Behind you, a grey wall is pouring down the valley.

To understand how you got here, we have to go back.

---

## DAY 1

**[DAY 1]**

Six twelve in the evening. Ember Valley. A quiet farm town at the foot of Mount Grael.
The mountain has slept for six hundred years.
Tonight, the plates on your shelf begin to rattle.

Then every phone in town buzzes at once.
"Ash fall expected. Stay indoors."

Across the road, your neighbours throw bags into their car and speed away.
They leave something behind.
A small brown dog with one floppy ear, sitting alone in the road.
You open the door. He runs straight in.
You call him Pebble.

First problem: the air.
Ash rides the wind. It gets in through windows, chimneys, even the kitchen fan.
So you close every window. You shut the chimney flue. You switch off every fan.
You roll wet towels along the bottom of the doors.

Nine forty. Something grey drifts past the porch light.
It looks like snow. It isn't.
You step on it, and it squeaks.
Volcanic ash isn't soft like campfire ash. It's tiny pieces of rock and glass.

Second problem: water.
Ash falls into rain barrels and tanks and makes the water dirty.
So tonight, before it gets worse, you fill every bottle, every pot, every jug.
One person needs about a gallon a day. Pebble needs some too.
You count. Thirty-one gallons.
You put the lid on the rain barrel, and a heavy stone on top.

---

## DAY 2

**[DAY 2]**

Seven in the morning. The world outside is grey. No birds. No cars. No sun.
The ruler on the windowsill says two centimetres.

And the back door is open a crack.
Pebble is out in the yard, sneezing and rolling in the ash.

Rule two: never go back outside for anything.
You go anyway.

Ash hits your face. It slips behind your glasses, and you rub your eye.
Don't.
Ash scratches an eye the way sand scratches a window. Rinse it with clean water instead.

You scoop up Pebble and run back in.
Your eye stings. Your glasses have a tiny scratch now, right in the middle.
And without your glasses, you can barely see your own hand.

So you make a promise. Glasses on. Always. Never contact lenses in ash.
You wipe Pebble's paws, one by one, so he doesn't lick the glass off them.
He licks your hand instead.

Evening. The ruler says six centimetres.
Thirty gallons. One scratch. One dog.
And above your bed, for the first time, the roof gives a long, slow creak.
