## COLD OPEN

If the sky flashes, but there's no rain and no thunder, stay inside.

**[DAY 10]**

That isn't a storm. It's lightning inside a cloud of ash.

You're under the kitchen table with a little brown dog shaking in your arms.
Every flash lights up the window.
And every flash shows the same thing.
Your dog isn't looking at the lightning.
He's staring up at the ridge above town.
Like he's waiting for someone.

Ten days ago, the mountain above your town woke up.
Your neighbours drove away and left their dog in the road.
You took him in. You called him Pebble.
You thought he was just a scared dog.
You were wrong.

---

## DAY 3

**[DAY 3]**

Seven in the morning. Ember Valley is grey.
Grey roofs. Grey fields. A grey sky with no sun in it.
The ash falls all day, soft and silent, like snow that never melts.

But it isn't snow.
Volcanic ash is tiny pieces of rock and glass.
It scratches your eyes. It hurts your lungs. It gets into everything.

So your house is sealed. Windows shut. Chimney closed. Wet towels under every door.

**First problem: water.**
Your water is in bottles, pots and jugs, lined up along the kitchen wall.
One person needs about a gallon a day.
You count them. Thirty gallons.
That's about a month, if nothing goes wrong.

The ruler on the windowsill says seven centimetres.

That night, at ten o'clock exactly, Pebble climbs onto the windowsill.
He doesn't look at the mountain.
He looks the other way. Up at Quarry Ridge.
And he whines. Softly. For an hour.

---

## DAY 4

**[DAY 4]**

Twenty-nine gallons. Eight centimetres.

At noon, the lights flicker. Once. Twice.
A bang down the road, and everything goes quiet.
The fridge stops humming. The clock on the oven goes dark.

Ash is heavy. When it gets damp, it can carry electricity.
It piles up on power lines and shorts them out.

You put a flashlight and a box of batteries on the table.
You count the batteries. Fourteen.
From now on, the flashlight is only for emergencies.

**Second problem: food.**
A closed fridge keeps food cold for about four hours.
A full freezer, about two days.
So today, you eat the fridge first.
Cheese. Milk. Last night's soup.
Pebble gets the sausages. He thinks this is the best day of his life.

Ten o'clock.
Pebble is back on the windowsill, staring at the ridge.

---

## DAY 6

**[DAY 6]**

Twenty-seven gallons. Nine centimetres. Eleven batteries.

Every time Pebble goes outside to the toilet, he comes back grey.
Grey paws. Grey belly. Grey ears.
And he licks it. All of it.

Don't let him.
Ash is glass. Keep pets inside as much as you can, and wipe the ash off their fur and paws.

So you kneel down with an old towel and clean him, paw by paw.
Then you rub the towel around his neck, under his red collar.
And something clinks.

A little metal tag. Under all that fur.
You've never noticed it before.
You turn it over and wipe off the ash.

It doesn't say your neighbours' name.
It doesn't say their address.
It says: "If found, return to Quarry Ridge Ranger Station."

Pebble was never your neighbours' dog.
They were only looking after him.
He belongs to someone up on the ridge.

Every night at ten, he hasn't been scared.
He's been waiting for them.

---

## DAY 9

**[DAY 9]**

Twenty-four gallons. Ten centimetres. Eleven batteries.

You wake up coughing.
The house is sealed, but ash still sneaks in. On boots. On fur. Through tiny gaps.

**Third problem: the air.**
In the workshop, under your grandfather's old toolbox, you find a box of dust masks.
The good kind, the ones that fit tight around your nose and mouth.
You count them. Twelve.

You could tie a wet cloth over your face instead.
But a damp cloth only helps a little. Ash is so fine it slips right through.
A mask that fits tight stops far more of it.

You put one on and breathe.
Then you clean the floor.
You mist the ash with a spray bottle first. Dry ash flies up into your face. Damp ash stays down.
And you don't wash it into the drain. Ash and water turn into sludge that blocks the pipes.

That evening, the ash cloud thins for a few minutes.
For the first time in days, you can see the top of Quarry Ridge.
The ranger station is up there. A tiny wooden roof.

And from its chimney, a thin line of smoke is rising.

Pebble sees it too.
He doesn't whine this time.
He barks. Once. Loud.
Someone is alive up there.

---

## DAY 10

**[DAY 10]**

Twenty-two gallons. Eleven masks.

The sky starts to flash.
No rain. No thunder. Just silent white light inside the grey.

Ash clouds can make their own lightning. Stay indoors.

Pebble hates it.
He shakes so hard his tags rattle.
So you crawl under the kitchen table and hold him until it stops.

Lying there, you look at the map on the wall.
Ember Valley. The river through the middle.
And on the other side of the river, the trail up to Quarry Ridge.

You could get there.
But not across the river valley.
When a volcano is awake, mud and ash can rush down a river valley, fast, with almost no warning.
If you go, you go the long way. High ground only.

---

## DAY 12

**[DAY 12]**

Twenty gallons. Twelve centimetres. Eleven masks.

Twelve centimetres of ash weighs more than you think.
It sits on every roof in Ember Valley, and it doesn't blow away.
And if it ever rains, wet ash can weigh about twice as much.

That night, lying in bed, you hear it.
A long, slow creak. Right above your head.
Then a tick. Then another.

You want to climb up there with a shovel and push it all off.
Don't.
Ash on a roof is slippery, like walking on marbles.
After eruptions, falling off a roof is one of the most common ways people get hurt.

So you do the safe thing.
You shine the flashlight at the ceiling.
No sagging. No cracks. Not yet.
And you make one more rule.
If the ceiling ever starts to bend, you leave that room, and you don't go back.

Ten o'clock.
Pebble goes to the window, like every night.
You stand next to him and look up at the ridge.

And this time, the smoke is gone.

## TEASE

No smoke. No light. Nothing.
Somebody up there kept a fire going for nine days.
Tonight, they stopped.
