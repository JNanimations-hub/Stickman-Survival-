**COMPANION:** **Pebble**, a small, scruffy brown dog with one floppy ear. Nico's neighbours left him behind when they drove away. **Secret (revealed Day 6):** his collar tag says "If found, return to Quarry Ridge Ranger Station". ---

## WORLD

- **Where:** Ember Valley, a made-up farm town at the foot of a made-up volcano, Mount Grael. A river runs through the middle of town. A ranger station sits up on Quarry Ridge.
- **What went wrong:** Mount Grael has slept for 600 years. On Day 1, it wakes up. The wind blows the ash cloud straight over Ember Valley, and it keeps falling for 100 days.
- **Rules of the danger:**
  - **Ash isn't soft.** It's tiny bits of rock and glass. It scratches eyes, hurts lungs and ruins machines.
  - **Ash is heavy.** It piles up on roofs, and rain makes it much heavier.
  - **Ash travels on air:** open windows, fans, chimneys, coats and dog fur.
  - **The river valley is a trap.** Mudflows from the volcano (lahars) can rush down it with little warning.
  - **What stops it:** sealed rooms, masks, glasses or goggles, high ground, and staying inside.

**THE RULES (for comment arguments)**
1. Never open a window, even when it's hot.
2. Never go back outside for anything. *(Nico breaks this on Day 3 to save Pebble. Was he right?)*
3. Never climb onto the roof.
4. Never drink the rain.
5. Never cross the valley.

## NARRATOR

- Always talks to **"you."**
- **Present tense:** "You open your eyes. The window is grey."
- **Calm and serious.** Short sentences.
- Uses the **numbered problem** pattern: "First problem: water."
- Uses the one-word warning: **"Don't."** Then explains why with a real fact.
- Reads the **countdown** out loud at each jump: "Nineteen gallons. Twelve days of food. Four masks."

## FACTS LIST (true, safe, practical; keep these apart from the story)

*Sources to check before publishing: USGS Volcano Hazards Program, IVHHN (International Volcanic Health Hazard Network), Ready.gov / FEMA.*

1. When ash fall is expected, go inside, close windows, doors and chimney flues, and turn off fans and air conditioners that pull in outside air.
2. Volcanic ash isn't like soft fire ash. It's made of tiny pieces of rock, minerals and volcanic glass.
3. Store drinking water early, about 1 gallon per person per day, and cover water tanks, barrels and wells so ash can't get in.
4. Wear glasses instead of contact lenses in ash, and goggles if you have them. Ash under a contact lens can scratch your eye.
5. If ash gets in your eyes, don't rub them. Rinse them with clean water.
6. Ash, especially wet ash, can short out power lines and cause power cuts. Keep flashlights and spare batteries ready.
7. Keep pets inside during ash fall. Wipe ash off their paws and fur so they don't lick it up.
8. A well-fitting N95-type mask gives good protection from ash. A damp cloth over your nose and mouth helps only a little.
9. Never climb onto a roof covered in ash. It's slippery, and falls from roofs cause many injuries after eruptions. Leave roof cleaning to adults with safety gear.
10. Signs a roof may be overloaded include sagging ceilings and creaking or cracking sounds. Stay out of those rooms.
11. Lightly mist ash with water before sweeping so it doesn't fly up into the air. Don't wash ash into drains; it can block them.
12. Wash garden fruit and vegetables well before eating. Wipe the outside of cans before opening them.
13. Ash clouds can make their own lightning. Stay indoors.
14. Rain makes ash much heavier, roughly up to twice as heavy. A few inches of wet ash can be enough to damage a weak roof.
15. Rain falling through an ash cloud can carry ash and chemicals. Don't drink it unless it has been properly treated.
16. Before you leave a shelter, leave a note with the date, where you're going and why.
17. Lahars (volcanic mudflows) can rush down river valleys very fast. During volcanic activity, stay out of valleys and get to high ground.
18. A basic emergency kit includes water, food, a flashlight, batteries, first aid, medicines, a whistle and a battery or hand-crank radio.
19. If supplies run low, cut back on food before water. Drink what you need today and look for more tomorrow.
20. Three of anything (three whistle blasts, three flashes, three fires) is a common signal for help.
21. Bright colours that contrast with the ground are easier for rescuers to see from the air.
22. If you're lost and people are looking for you, staying in one place with a signal usually makes you easier to find.
23. In a power cut, a closed fridge keeps food cold for about 4 hours, and a full closed freezer for about 48 hours. Eat fridge food first.
